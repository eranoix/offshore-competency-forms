## 2.29.0 — The rotation, run like a calendar
_2026-09-25_

- Every button at the top says what it does, in words, at every screen width: Flights count, Plan, Google Calendar, Print, Share and Shortcuts. Print, Share and Google Calendar are menus that open under their own button — seven named buttons do not fit on a laptop's line, and a second line takes height from every panel.
- Your Google Calendar on this calendar. Paste its secret address in iCal format once (Google Calendar → Settings → your calendar → Integrate calendar) and its events are drawn here in their own colour, fetched again on every visit, so a change made in Google shows here. It is fetched through the site with your session only, from Google, Outlook or iCloud and nowhere else, and the address stays in your own plan — not in a shared link, a QR code or the calendar you give out. An exported .ics file can also be imported once, and importing it twice adds nothing twice.
- The calendar starts with nothing on it. Holidays used to appear on their own; now the country's holidays are offered as a suggestion — a list to tick, with Add and No thanks — and only the ones taken go on, every year. Import has its own button, with Google Calendar, Outlook and Apple Calendar side by side, each with the steps to find its address in its own settings, and a calendar file for a one-off copy.
- Events are managed the way Google Calendar manages them. Click one and it opens — what it is, when it starts and ends — with Edit, Duplicate and Delete. Delete acts at once, from the button or the Delete key, and the toast that follows carries Undo (Ctrl/⌘+Z too). What is deleted goes to a bin in the Plan for thirty days, with Restore.
- Drag an event to move it; drag either end to change only its start or its end. Drag across days to pick a stretch. The day under the pointer is measured by position: the days have a gap between them, and a drag that began in it used to move the event twice as far as the hand did.
- Type an event the way you would say it — “Time off 8 to 13 sep”, “course 28/9 for 5 days”, “Ana's birthday 20/09 every year #family” — and what was understood shows as you type. More options opens the full form. Six categories with their own colours, and the legend turns each on and off. Every day takes a note.
- An event that comes round every year asks, when one of them is deleted or moved, whether it means only this year, this year and after, or every year.
- The calendar has three views — Calendar, Agenda and five years — and never a scrollbar: the agenda and the month's stretches are paged by what fits, measured after drawing. Days aboard can be worked as days, nights or a swing, and someone else's rotation can sit beside yours, with the next stretch you are both home.
- The dashboard: a countdown to the next crew change at the top (and on the installed app's icon), what the days pay — by the day, or a salary with the days sold on top, found from the calendar rather than typed — the certificates against the turns, with the last day at home to renew one that runs out aboard, and every count set against last month.
- The rotation as a calendar subscription for Google, Apple or Outlook. The reminders ride inside it as alarms that the phone's own calendar rings; a countdown rides in it as an all-day event on each of the next fourteen days, which is how it reaches the calendar widget on the home screen. Stop sharing switches the old address off — measured: a publicly cached feed kept answering for half an hour after, so it is never cached.
- Sharing sends only the rotation and its dates, compressed: the link used to carry the whole plan, pay and notes included, and its QR code was too dense to scan off a screen. Print asks which sheet: the turns, or the year on one page of A4, readable in black and white.

Six ports in it look British, and the column is as often the port sailed from, so they are flagged and never counted against anything. Thirty-five absences since December 2019 have no port written down at all, which is the larger hole in the evidence. And the very first row's days-in-hand is in calendar days where every row below it is in half-days — the doubling is already made once, on the first line of the sheet that defines the rule.
- The one test the book has never made: a single unbroken spell in the United Kingdom over 183 days ends the period on its own, whatever the arithmetic says. The longest is 87 days, so it has never bitten — it is reported now instead of being absent.
- The tracker is a template too. Fixed by saying the rotation sheet's rules more specifically rather than by loosening the forms' rule, which four documents depend on.

The Account page counted every document in the store and called them saved trips; there have been CAAP packs in that number for a long time and there are rotation plans in it now, so it says saved documents. The offline copy carried one published form while three were published — it is the fallback for a laptop at sea with no signal, so a stale one means somebody offshore opens the company original instead of the form that replaced it.
